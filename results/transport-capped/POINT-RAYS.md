# Point rays, actual chambers and the missing hypothesis

A complete common-type contraction and an exact whole-LR caution. No worldwide novelty claim is made.

## A complete common-type contraction lemma

Let C be a rational cone of feasible integral LR boundary data, in a fixed original hive chart. Assume a complete finite set of linear vertex maps v_i(b) describes the ENTIRE hive H_b=conv_i v_i(b) at every b in C, including its walls. Assume the normal fan is fixed on the relative interior and the maps remain feasible on C; equivalently the same complete maps supply Minkowski linearity on C. The qualification is mathematical, not a label inferred from a stored chamber record.

For b,a in C, the complete normal-fan support functions are linear in the boundary on every normal cell. Thus H_(b+a)=H_b+H_a. This also follows by grouping the corresponding support-maximizing vertex maps; it does not follow from the universal inclusion alone. If H_a is a single integral point z, adding a translates the entire hive by z and preserves all its stretching coefficients.

More generally, suppose C is generated by feasible integral point directions a_1,...,a_s and other feasible integral directions v_1,...,v_l. Write b=sum x_i a_i+sum y_j v_j with nonnegative rational coefficients. Choose a positive integer L clearing all coefficients. The point contribution in H_(Lb) is an integral translation, and complete Minkowski linearity gives

    H_(Lb)=integer_point+sum_j (L*y_j) H_(v_j).

The right-hand sum uses the ORIGINAL lattice, not a quotient lattice inferred from rational ray coordinates. Consequently the entire period-one polynomial P_b is determined by the non-point summands: equality at every multiple Lt determines the whole polynomial. For l=1,

    P_b(t)=P_v(y_1*t).

Positive coefficients of P_v therefore prove all coefficients on the full admitted cone. Rational y_1 does not require an unsupported integer-point translation at every original grade; denominator clearing followed by LR period collapse supplies the extension. If all rays are point directions, the whole polynomial is one. For several non-point directions, mixed coefficients remain separate obligations.

This is useful only when the complete vertex/fan contract is proved. The result requires a fully established geometric chamber, including all its walls.

## An explicit whole-LR failure without the chamber hypothesis

For nonnegative integers x,y consider

    lambda=(2x+y,x+y,y), mu=(x+y,x), nu=(x+y,y).

The entire LR polynomial is

    P_(x,y)(t)=1+min(x,y)*t.                                  (1)

Here is the full tableau proof. The skew rows have lengths x,y,y. The first row is forced to contain x ones. Put k twos in the second row, so its remaining y-k entries are ones. The third row then has y-k twos and k ones. Semistandardness and the lattice word hold exactly when 0<=k<=min(x,y). The condition k<=x both prevents a one below the second row's overlap and prevents a two above a third-row cell, and is also the lattice-word bound while reading the third row. No further choice remains. Replacing x and y by x*t and y*t proves (1) at every nonnegative integer stretch.

Both coordinate rays are point families, but their sum x=y=1 has polynomial 1+t. Thus merely knowing every generating ray has polynomial one does not give Minkowski equality or whole positivity by point deletion. The real family has two chambers x>=y and y>=x; on either complete chamber the contraction lemma gives the correct internal diagonal ray and formula. This is an obstruction to the shortcut, not a new negative LR candidate.

## What the external-column paper actually supplies

Stefan Trandafir's External Columns and Chambers of Vector Partition Functions (published online 3 November 2025, DCG 75 (2026), 141-192) proves a reduction for an ACTUAL vector-partition matrix A, an actual chamber, and saturation of the semigroup of its external columns in the column lattice. Removing k external rows/columns preserves the fiber dimension n-d; it reduces the presentation, not automatically the sign problem. Its polynomial external-chamber case gives the positive binomial expression after the exact hypotheses hold.

Section 9.1.1 discusses LR as a vector-partition-like function and gives a rank-four computed example. It does not prove that its general matrix theorem applies to every LR polynomiality chamber. The first arm therefore retains the common-type lemma and the explicit missing-hypothesis falsifier, while deferring any global external-column LR reduction until the actual matrix/saturation/common-chamber bridge is supplied. The complete 3-by-5 normal route has much smaller verified obligations than a fresh whole rank-six chamber census and supplies the companion whole-family certificate.
