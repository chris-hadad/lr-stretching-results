# The ordinary linear coefficient of every two-row Kostka family

Root derivation, Astra006, 13 September 2026. The disjoint-row character setup
extends the accepted Pro027/root slope-two and slope-three work. The general
hinge formula, concavity argument and uniform nonpoint bound below are new root
work in this session. No worldwide-priority or human-review claim is made.

## Entire object and admissibility

Let w_1,...,w_n be nonnegative integers, W their sum, and let k be an integer
with 0<=k<=W/2 and w_i<=W-k for every i. These are exactly the dominance
conditions for the nonzero two-row Kostka number of shape (W-k,k) and content
w. The corresponding entire stretching polynomial is

    P(t) = K_((W-k)t,kt),(tw_1,...,tw_n).

It is also an entire ordinary LR polynomial: set lambda_i=sum_(j>=i)w_j,
mu_i=lambda_(i+1), and nu=(W-k,k), trimming trailing zeros. Column-disjoint
skew rows give skew Schur character product_i h_(tw_i). The two-variable
highest-weight extraction is

    P(t) = [x^(kt)] (1-x) product_i (1+x+...+x^(tw_i)).

Zero weights may be retained or deleted. The all-zero input and the one-label
case are points. An inadmissible nonempty input is outside the theorem and
has its separate zero-polynomial convention.

## Complete linear formula

Assume n>=2 and put d=n-2. For a subset J write w(J)=sum_(i in J)w_i and
j=|J|. Admissibility implies w(J)>=k whenever j=n-1: its complement is a
single weight at most W-k. The same is true at j=n. Thus only j<=d can
contribute in the complete inclusion-exclusion expansion:

    P(t) = binom(kt+d,d)
           + sum_(nonempty J, w(J)<k) (-1)^j
               binom((k-w(J))t+d-j,d).

For j<=d, every upper entry here is a nonnegative integer at t>=0. The
polynomial binomial agrees with the combinatorial zero convention at small
grades. Subsets of weight at least k contribute zero at every positive
grade, and the nonempty displayed terms have zero constant. This is an
all-t count identity before coefficient extraction, not an interpolation.

Let H_m=sum_(a=1)^m 1/a, with H_0=0. The first term has linear coefficient
k H_d. The numerator of a nonempty term has one zero factor, j-1 negative
integer factors and d-j positive integer factors. Its linear coefficient,
including the inclusion-exclusion sign, is

    -(k-w(J)) (j-1)!(d-j)!/d!.

Consequently the complete ordinary coefficient is

    c1(w,k) = k H_d
        - sum_(J, 1<=|J|<=d) (k-w(J))_+ / (|J| binom(d,|J|)).       (1)

For d=0 the sum and H_0 are zero, as required by the two-label point family.
At k=0 the same formula is zero. Padding with zero labels changes its form
but not its value, because the complete count identity remains the same.

Every coefficient of the subtracted hinge functions is positive. Formula (1)
therefore defines a concave, positively homogeneous function of the real
parameters (w,k). On rational admissible parameters it is the homogeneous
extension of the actual ordinary coefficient by denominator clearing. No
Ehrhart polynomial of an irrational polytope is asserted. This is a special
family result; the accepted counterexample to general LR c1 concavity remains.

## Nonnegativity from the complete admissible cone

Introduce u_0=W-2k and u_i=w_i for i>=1. Put S=(sum_i u_i)/2=W-k.
Admissibility is precisely

    u_i>=0 and u_i<=S for every i.

The complete polynomial is the multiplicity of the trivial SL(2)
representation in product_i Sym^(t u_i), including the added u_0. This is
the usual self-dual highest-weight identity: tensoring by Sym^(t u_0)
selects its multiplicity in the original tensor product. Formula (1),
transported by this linear change of variables, remains concave and
positively homogeneous; call it G(u).

The polygon cone above is generated by e_i+e_j for i!=j. A direct proof is
to partition a circle of circumference 2S into consecutive intervals of
lengths u_i and pair each point with its translate by S. An interval of
length at most S contains no positive-measure set of antipodal pairs.
If a_ij is the measure of points in interval i whose antipodes lie in
interval j, then a_ij=a_ji>=0, a_ii=0 and sum_j a_ij=u_i. Thus

    u = sum_(i<j) a_ij(e_i+e_j).

The edge weights are rational for rational interval lengths. S=0 is separate.
Each generator has the constant stretching polynomial one, hence G=0.
Concavity and positive homogeneity imply superadditivity:

    G(u+v) >= G(u)+G(v).

The complete generator decomposition therefore proves G(u)>=0 on the
entire admissible cone. This proves c1>=0 for every two-row Kostka family,
at every rank and size, with its entire ordinary LR realization.

## Exact zero classification and a uniform integer lower bound

Write m for the number of positive augmented weights u_i. If m<=3, the
feasible SL(2) invariant has multiplicity one at every stretch, by the
multiplicity-free two-factor Clebsch-Gordan rule and the even total weight.
If some u_i=S, the other factors have total highest weight S; their highest
component occurs exactly once and is the only one pairing with Sym^(tS)
to the trivial representation. Again P=1.

In every other case, m>=4 and every positive u_i<=S-1. The integer S is at
least two. Choose four positive coordinates containing every coordinate
equal to S-1. Such a choice always exists: at S=2 there are exactly four
positive unit coordinates; at S>=3 three coordinates equal to S-1 plus a
fourth positive coordinate would already exceed the total 2S.

Subtract one from each chosen coordinate. The residual vector u' is
nonnegative and has half-total S-2. Every chosen coordinate is at most S-2
after subtraction; every unchosen coordinate was already at most S-2.
Thus u' remains in the polygon cone, including the all-zero possibility.
The removed four-unit vector v has P_v(t)=t+1: pair two copies of Sym^t,
whose t+1 distinct irreducible summands each pair once with the other pair.
Superadditivity now gives

    c1(P_u) = G(u) >= G(u')+G(v) >= 1.                         (2)

Therefore c1=0 exactly in the point cases above, and every nonconstant
admissible two-row Kostka stretching polynomial has c1>=1. The constant one
is sharp, attained by the four-unit invariant family. This is an absolute
ordinary coefficient bound, not a normalized relative margin.

## Sharp all-unit comparison in the lower-slope regime

For positive integer w_i, k>=2 and n>=2k, each term (k-w(J))_+ in (1) is
maximized at all w_i=1. A non-unit weight strictly decreases at least its
singleton term, so the all-unit profile uniquely minimizes c1. Summing by
subset size and using d=n-2 gives the exact minimum

    c1_min = k H_(n-2)
      - n(n-1) sum_(j=1)^(k-1) (k-j)/(j(n-j)(n-j-1))
      = k-1 + k(H_(n-k-1)-H_(k-1)) >= k-1.

The second equality follows from the partial fractions

    (k-j)/(j(n-j)(n-j-1))
      = k/[n(n-1)j] + (n-k)/[n(n-j)]
        - (n-k-1)/[(n-1)(n-j-1)].

This proves the linear comparison at all such n and k. The independently
enumerated slope-four ranks 8 through 40 and slope-five ranks 10 through 24
also have complete higher-coefficient all-unit comparisons, but their
all-n/all-k higher-coefficient extension is not proved by concavity of c1.

## Exact next coefficient, with its obstruction retained

The same extraction yields, for d>=2,

    c2 = k^2 (H_d^2-sum_(a=1)^d 1/a^2)/2
      - sum_(J,1<=j<=d) (k-w(J))_+^2
          (H_(d-j)-H_(j-1))/(j binom(d,j)).

The harmonic difference changes sign with j. The c1 concavity proof therefore
does not transfer by declaring all quadratic hinges convex with the same
sign. This complete functional is a next discriminator for a stronger
two-row theorem; no universal c2 or whole-polynomial positivity follows here.
