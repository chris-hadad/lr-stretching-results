# Lower proofs retaining a complete two-coordinate fiber

Let the complete fifth-interior model of an actual nine-dimensional saturated hive chart be a finite integral H-system. Split its coordinates into seven fixed separator coordinates and two remaining coordinates. For each explicitly selected integer separator state s, retain EVERY original inequality and a finite integer rectangle in the two remaining coordinates. Enumerating the first coordinate and intersecting the exact second-coordinate integer intervals imposed by all rows counts precisely the points of that entire restricted two-dimensional fiber. No product of one-dimensional marginal counts is used. In particular mixed coefficients, corner cuts, integer rounding and every boundary constant remain present.

Different separator states define disjoint subsets of the whole interior. Consequently the sum of these complete restricted fiber counts is a rigorous lower bound on I(5). Unselected states remain unknown. The rectangle can be any declared finite rectangle; proof of its exhaustive coverage of the full fiber is unnecessary for a lower bound. A whole-count equality would additionally require a complete separator-state and fiber-domain cover, which this experiment does not supply.

The producer uses necessary integer propagation and a midpoint traversal to find promising states. The independent verifier does not import that search or either numerical LR model. It checks the full row digest and body supplied by the authenticated geometry, the fixed separator coordinates, integer rectangle endpoints and exact distinctness of all separator keys. For every first-coordinate value, rational lower/upper endpoints from every original inequality give the second-coordinate interval; independent rational floor/ceiling operations verify its cardinality. Summing those exact finite fibers validates the claimed lower bound. Search cutoffs affect only which fibers are offered, not validity of accepted proof pieces.

This is a specific complete-joint-state repair of the earlier one-axis lower subsets, useful when individual intervals require many proof pieces. It is not ordinary positivity under a moving summation, a proof that all separator states are independent, a new entire polynomial, or an entire separator contraction. Its actual proof-size and runtime measurements are recorded separately on identical already certified pilot parents. The original complete chart, row–hive map, degree, lattice and global coefficient identities remain the whole-LR bridge. Numerical or proof results in this experiment do not increase global sign coverage.
