## Integer-infeasibility proof objects

The new source produces a finite proof tree for each certified empty strict system. Each deduction names an original fixed-grade row and a proposed integral lower or upper coordinate bound. Its premises are previously proved bounds on the other coordinates. Maximizing those terms gives a valid necessary one-coordinate inequality; rounding it inward to an integer endpoint is sound. A leaf finishes with a negative maximal row value or contradictory lower and upper bounds. A branch partitions a proved finite integer coordinate interval into **every** individual value and supplies a proof for every child. No branch may be omitted or replaced by a work-limit refusal.

The separate checker does not search or count. It validates a proposed lower endpoint v by the two exact inequalities a*(v-1)+S<0<=a*v+S, and an upper endpoint by a*(v+1)+S<0<=a*v+S. Thus it does not reuse the searcher's floor-division implementation. It checks all leaf contradictions, every branch endpoint and complete child cardinality. A positive outcome is instead an explicit point checked in every row. Search limits and unresolved cases remain explicit.

These are complete finite integer-infeasibility derivations. Their consequences do not rest only on a counter returning the number zero. The proof rule is the elementary maximization/rounding implication above, and every use is exposed in the saved proof. Source, implementation and model independence remain distinct: the checker is an independent arithmetic replay of a hive-based mathematical proof, not a different numerical interior model.

## Entire-polynomial reconstruction

Suppose an explicit strict point proves dimension D, and complete derivations prove emptiness of the true interiors at grades 1,..., k. Ehrhart reciprocity for the period-one LR polynomial gives the exact roots -1,...,-k. Consequently

    P(t)=R_k(t)*Q(t),       R_k(t)=product(j=1..k)(t+j),
    degree Q <= D-k.

At nonnegative integers s, R_k(s)>0. Therefore the complete values P(s)/R_k(s) at s=0,...,D-k determine Q in its **prior proved** degree space. The resulting product determines every ordinary coefficient of the entire LR polynomial. It is not a low-degree guess based on small positive values. Constant one is used only for a proved nonempty family; a strict point already supplies nonemptiness at a positive grade. Empty/lower-dimensional charts without such a point do not receive these root premises and retain the original full-positive-node fallback.

The two reserved positive checks are s=D-k+1 and D-k+2, frozen from the proved space before new count execution and taken in separate scalar calls. They are not used to choose degree, k, parity or an interpolation fit. Complete vectors are persisted immediately after determination and before holdouts or model comparisons; all coefficient signs remain part of the certificate.

Both the complete H-system counter and the independent complete LR row-tableau recurrence compute every **positive determining and held value** used for a consequential repaired certificate. They share the explicitly proved geometric negative-root premises, just as both share the original degree and LR polynomiality premises. Their independent finite counts are not claimed to be independently generated interior counts. A separate rational solve checks the full mixed-node polynomial against the root-and-quotient arithmetic. A complete certificate requires every root proof, strict point, degree premise, count, holdout, full coefficient vector and identity binding.

If k>=D-1, the already-adopted high-codegree theorem is a direct positivity terminal: the residual Q is linear or constant, and positive leading volume plus P(0)=1 give positive factors. This can replace numerical fitting. For smaller k, the roots alone do not imply signs; the complete Q and its product must still be determined. In particular Reeve-type examples show why high codegree insufficient for a linear/constant residual must not be promoted to positivity without additional proof.

## Computational purpose of the reconstruction

The initial reconstruction tests on identical inputs measure the cost of full positive grades 0,..., D. Some bound 10–12 cases refuse at high grades. The mathematical repair proves the missing negative-grade zeros before changing the determining space, thereby avoiding some expensive positive grades while retaining an entire-polynomial certificate. It supplies a concrete reduction of the determining degree space; it is not an assertion that arbitrary partial observations determine all signs. The finite derivations and measurements determine its useful scope, not a presumed global codegree law.
