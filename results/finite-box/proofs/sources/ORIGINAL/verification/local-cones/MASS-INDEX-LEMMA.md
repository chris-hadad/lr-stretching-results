# A lattice-index bound for complete coordinate-identification hives

Root derivation for Frontier025 return verification, 10 September 2026.
This is a mathematical addition proposed for independent review, with no
claim of worldwide novelty.

Let the entire hive have a full-dimensional saturated chart in `Z^d` obtained
by assigning each original coordinate an integral boundary-linear offset plus
one selected coordinate, or an offset alone. Retain every original rhombus.
Each row normal has positive coordinate mass at most two and negative mass at
most two. Primitive reduction preserves those bounds. Every actual facet is
supported by a retained row, so the bounds hold for primitive facet normals.

For any such row `a`, append a ground coordinate `-sum(a)`. The resulting
zero-sum vector has total positive mass, equal to its total negative mass,
at most two. Pair its positive and negative unit entries. It is a sum of at
most two directed incidence roots `e_i-e_j`. Deleting the ground coordinate
recovers `a`.

Take any `r` independent normals and any `r` columns. Multilinearity expands
the corresponding determinant into at most `2^r` determinants of incidence
submatrices. Each such determinant is zero, one or minus one by total
unimodularity of directed incidence matrices and their transposes. Consequently
every `r`-minor has absolute value at most `2^r`. The index of the lattice
generated by the rows in its saturated real span is the positive gcd of the
nonzero minors, hence is at most `2^r`. In particular every two-normal plane
index is at most four, independently of the dimension.

Source proof 023 gives the complete indexed two-cone BV weight

\[
 \alpha=\frac14+s(p,q)
 -\frac{\langle u,v\rangle(\|u\|^{-2}+\|v\|^{-2})}{12q}.
\]

Its norm-six estimate bounds the numerator of the last term by `12/5`, and
the finite Dedekind sum is at least `-(q-1)(q-2)/(12q)`. Using the stronger
membership-specific restriction `q<=4`, the respective lower bounds are
`1/20, 3/20, 23/180, 3/40`. Thus every codimension-two weight of an admitted
full chart is at least `1/20`. With the same BV face formula and rational
period-one dilation argument as source proof 023,

\[
 [t^{d-2}]L_P(t)\;\ge\;\frac1{20}
       \sum_{\dim F=d-2}\operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb Z}(F)>0.
\]

This strengthens the quantitative bound only for the specified whole-chart
membership. Norm-six polytopes without the mass hypothesis retain the source
bound `1/100`. Hidden lower-dimensional members require their own saturated
chart; a coordinate upper bound alone does not establish this hypothesis.

The mass and index restrictions do not force positive codimension-three
weights. The three normals `(2,-1,-1,0)`, `(1,-2,0,0)`, `(1,-1,0,0)` all meet
the two-mass condition. They are a common coordinate reflection of source
proof 031's example. In their saturated three-plane the normal determinant
is minus one, and primitive tangent rays are `(0,0,-1)`, `(-1,-1,-1)`,
`(2,1,3)`. Their squared lengths are `1,3,14`, with pairwise products
`1,-3,-6`. The complete unimodular three-cone formula gives

\[
 \alpha=\frac18+\frac1{24}
 \left\{\frac43-\frac{45}{14}-\frac{17}{7}\right\}
 =-\frac{55}{1008}.
\]

Thus even index one permits a negative local three-cone constant. Actual
whole-hive incidence and the sum over all relevant faces remain essential;
no entire LR negative coefficient is supplied by these three rows alone.

There is a concrete obstruction to transferring the return's negative Reeve
example directly into this class. Its two-pyramid simplex has vertices
`0,e1,e2,(1,1,m,0,0),e4,e5` in `Z^5`. Two primitive inward facet normals are
`(m,0,-1,0,0)` and `(0,0,1,0,0)`. Their common face is
`conv(0,e2,e4,e5)`, of dimension three, so this is an actual codimension-two
face. The gcd of their nonzero two-minors is `m`. This index is invariant
under affine lattice equivalence, since its linear part induces a lattice
isomorphism on the dual saturated plane.

For `m>=5` the simplex therefore cannot be affine-lattice-equivalent to a
full-dimensional five-coordinate identification hive. In particular the
negative `m=47` example lacks this direct geometric bridge. This excludes
neither another LR count realization with the same polynomial nor an entire
hive using a different kind of saturated chart. It is not a whole-LR
positivity theorem, and it does not exclude negative low coefficients in
larger or more general LR families.
