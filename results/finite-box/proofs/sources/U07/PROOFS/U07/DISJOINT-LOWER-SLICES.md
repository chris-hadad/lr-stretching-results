# Lower-interior certificates from explicit disjoint complete slices

A negative sufficient c1 lower bound at target 149499 reveals that upper covers alone are not the needed repair at degree eight. Its initial full coefficient-space identities require I4 at least 582 for a strict c1 certificate; the first finite cover has proven lower 201 and upper 3939. This does not establish a negative c1 and does not determine the exact count.

In the already checked saturated eight-coordinate chart, fix seven integer coordinates and retain the full interval of the eighth allowed by every original fourth-interior inequality. For a proposed interval [l,h], the minimum of each affine row is attained at one endpoint; direct integer substitution proves its entire feasibility. Its cardinality is h-l+1. Two such intervals with unequal seven-coordinate prefixes are disjoint. Their total cardinality therefore gives a rigorous lower bound on the complete I4, without completing the remaining prefix search or assuming any omitted fiber empty.

The search may use necessary interval propagation, an ordering of fixed coordinates and midpoint-first traversal to find useful slices. None of those heuristics is a proof premise: the independent checker sees only the explicitly supplied intervals, verifies every endpoint minimum and exact integer prefix distinctness, and adds their cardinalities. It imports neither the search nor a numerical count oracle. Stopping as soon as a required lower threshold is reached leaves an explicitly partial lower certificate, not an exact count. One may truncate the last fully feasible interval to reach a threshold exactly; that says nothing about the sharpness of the resulting lower bound.

Intersect this lower bound with the protected-coefficient interval and the independently replayed full upper cover. The seven-count octic functional then proves complete signs whenever all five unprotected affine numerator minima are positive. An insufficient interval leaves the target unresolved unless a separate full-vector certificate supplies complete determining values and unused checks. A complete lower witness proof is not a second numerical I4 computation, and a sufficient lower bound does not produce a full coefficient vector.

This is the precise lower-bound repair of the adaptive count-enclosure route, on the entire original interior lattice. It is not a theorem of positivity under summing moving ordinary-positive slices, and no LR rank is assigned to an individual slice. The construction is valid for any finite integral H-system supplied with these explicit feasible intervals.
