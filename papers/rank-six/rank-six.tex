\begin{abstract}
\setlength{\emergencystretch}{1em}
We prove coefficient positivity at partition length at most six by an exact
computer-assisted argument for stretched Littlewood--\allowbreak Richardson
polynomials. The argument uses the original hive lattice at every boundary and actual
dimension. Its decisive step is a realizability restriction on a complete
refined normal-cycle certificate: a normal support whose universal tight-row
closure has excessive interior rank cannot carry a face of the required
dimension. Its sign constraint may therefore be omitted, while the complete
balanced coefficient identity is retained. In rank six this permits an exact
rational correction of every contributing edge weight and yields
\(c_1\geq (1/2000000)\sum_E\vol(E)\).
We assemble the higher-coefficient certificates in the same conventions and
give a small independent computation of the top coefficients. The complete
finite checks establish strict positivity of every coefficient through the
actual degree for a nonempty hive. We separate the geometric
proof, exact finite premises, certificate verification, and regeneration costs.
The account also explains the scalar-compression and lattice algorithms that
make the computation practical, and records the substantial AI contributions
and the limits of their independent checks.
We additionally prove a constructive legal-realization converse for retained
physical branches and give a complete 103-identity presentation of rank-six
hive-face closure, certified by short propositional proofs.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement and mathematical contribution}
Let \(\lambda,\mu,\nu\) be nonnegative integral partitions with
\(|\lambda|=|\mu|+|\nu|\). Throughout, \(\lambda\) is the outer partition.
For positive integers \(t\), write
\[
 P_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}(t)=c^{t\lambda}_{t\mu,t\nu}.
\]
These values agree with a polynomial in \(t\) by stretching polynomiality
\cite{rassart}. The coefficients discussed here are its ordinary monomial
coefficients, rather than its values at integers or coefficients in a binomial
basis. The King--Tollu--Toumazet conjecture \cite{ktt} asserts their nonnegativity without
a restriction on the partition lengths.

\begin{theorem}[Rank-six coefficient positivity]\label{thm:main}
Suppose that the maximum trimmed length of \(\lambda,\mu,\nu\) is at most
six, and let \(H\) be their original hive polytope. If \(H\) is nonempty
and \(d=\dim H\), then
\[
 P_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{d}c_k t^k,
 \qquad c_0=1,\qquad c_k>0\quad(1\leq k\leq d).
\]
All coefficients above \(d\) vanish. An empty positive-stretch family has
the zero polynomial. A point hive, including the all-empty triple, has
polynomial one.
\end{theorem}

The strengthened strict statement uses the small top-coefficient computation
in Section~\ref{sec:finite} together with the lower-coefficient certificates.
It is an assembly of analytic theorems and exact finite computations, not a
formal proof checked by a proof assistant. The release includes every
finite input and verifier identified in Section~\ref{sec:verification}.

For an actual face \(F\), \(\vol(F)\) denotes Lebesgue measure normalized
by the saturated lattice \(\Z^{10}\cap\operatorname{lin}(F-F)\); it is not
multiplied by \((\dim F)!\). More precisely, the proof gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounds}
 c_k(H)\ \geq\ \eps_k\sum_{\substack{F\text{ an actual face of }H\\\dim F=k}}
 \vol(F),\qquad 1\leq k\leq9,
\end{equation}
with
\[
\begin{aligned}
(\eps_1,\ldots,\eps_6)&=
\left(\frac1{2000000},\frac1{200000000},\frac1{200000000},
\frac1{2000000},\frac1{2000000},\frac1{3000}\right),\\
(\eps_7,\eps_8,\eps_9)&=
\left(\frac1{144},\frac1{9},\frac1{2}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
When \(d=10\), \(c_{10}=\vol(H)\). When \(d<k\), both the coefficient
and the face sum in \eqref{eq:bounds} vanish.

\subsection{Why a further step was needed}
Ferudun proved positivity through length five \cite{ferudun}. His proof
already uses exact normal corrections and the closure relations of two-face
polygons. The present work builds on that geometric approach and the
Berline--Vergne local Euler--Maclaurin formula \cite{bv}.
Pointwise nonnegativity of the uncorrected local weights is unavailable:
negative local weights occur even on actual hives in the five-part case.
Thus positive integer counts, polynomiality, and positivity of the highest
coefficients do not by themselves settle the remaining coefficients.

At length six the ambient hive dimension is ten. The linear coefficient
involves nine-dimensional normal cones and corrections on eight-normal
supports. Direct enumeration of boundary triples is an infinite problem;
even a complete bounded census cannot prove the all-size assertion. The
finite normal formulation removes that infinity but leaves a substantial
exact lattice calculation and a large correction system. Our earlier
unpruned correction search did not find a feasible field. This failure was
not a proof that its sufficient system was infeasible.

The decisive observation is that many independent normal supports cannot
contribute to an actual hive edge. Their tightness forces an additional
independent interior equality. The new restriction is justified by an exact
geometric lemma; it does not discard a constraint merely because its value
is negative. The complete correction identity continues to hold before any
sign condition is applied.

\subsection{What the proof adds and what it uses}
The principal contributions assembled here are the family-dependent
realizability criterion, a complete refinement-and-balance argument applicable
to hidden affine strata, the exact rank-six correction fields, and practical
original-lattice algorithms for their construction and verification.
The scalar computation also uses an odd-dimensional reciprocity reduction
with a finite-character correction for nontrivial lattice cosets.
The strengthened geometry identifies precisely which independent physical
branches occur for some legal integral boundary and compatible refinement,
at any rank. At rank six, a 103-identity calculus determines every tight-row
closure; a small propositional certificate proves its completeness without
an extreme-ray completeness oracle. The earlier 102 short identities first
fail at a generating set of cardinality six.
These are explicit constructions and specializations of classical local
Euler--Maclaurin, lattice, reciprocity, and finite Fourier methods; no claim
to have originated those classical methods is intended.

The finite-box result for seven parts and outer size at most thirty is a
separate theorem. The collection preserves its proof and compares the
recent finite-cover preprint of Ghodsi \cite{ghodsi}. Neither bounded result
is a premise of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. The theorem here leaves ordinary
rank seven and unrestricted KTT positivity open. Novelty comparisons are
bounded literature checks, not a claim of exhaustive worldwide priority.

\section{Original hive, lattice, and local constants}\label{sec:model}
Put \(T_6=\{(i,j)\in\Z_{\geq0}^2:i+j\leq6\}\), and pad all partitions
to six parts. For a hive height function \(h:T_6\to\R\), use boundary values
\[
 h(i,0)=\sum_{a=1}^i\mu_a,\qquad
 h(0,j)=\sum_{a=1}^j\lambda_a,\qquad
 h(6-j,j)=|\mu|+\sum_{a=1}^j\nu_a.
\]
The balance identity makes the corner values consistent. The ten free
coordinates are the heights at
\[
(1,1),(1,2),(1,3),(1,4),(2,1),(2,2),(2,3),(3,1),(3,2),(4,1).
\]
Their lattice is \(L=\Z^{10}\), with its standard Euclidean metric.
For every elementary rhombus the sum at the two obtuse corners minus the
sum at the two acute corners is nonnegative. All 45 inequalities are kept.
After fixing the boundary they have the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rhombi}
 \rho_r(b,x)=\ell_r(b)+\langle n_r,x\rangle\geq0,
 \qquad 0\leq r<45,
\end{equation}
where \(b=(\lambda,\mu,\nu)\). Their interior restrictions give 42
distinct oriented primitive normal directions. Three directions have two
physical row preimages. Equal directions do not identify their boundary
offsets.

The hive correspondence identifies the integer points of \(H_b\) with
the LR coefficient \cite{kt}; homogeneous boundary scaling gives
\(H_{tb}=tH_b\) for \(t>0\). Stretching polynomiality implies that its Ehrhart
quasipolynomial has period one. The actual degree is \(\dim H_b\), which
may be smaller than ten. Padding is carried out in this original model;
no coordinate upper bound is substituted for the actual dimension.

For a rational hive polytope, choose a positive integer \(s\) clearing all its
vertices. Its integral dilate satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scaling}
 c_k(sH)=s^k c_k(H),\qquad \vol(sF)=s^k\vol(F).
\end{equation}
These identities use period-one polynomiality, not continuity of arbitrary
Ehrhart coefficients under perturbation. The integral case has constant term
one; hence so does every nonempty period-one rational hive. This also
discharges the point and all-empty cases.

\begin{definition}[Complete local constant]
For an independent set \(I\) of \(q\) original normals, let
\(\sigma_I=\pos(n_i:i\in I)\). Its constant \(\alpha_I\) is the
Berline--Vergne dual local value at zero, with the saturated normal lattice
\(L^*\cap\operatorname{span}(\sigma_I)\) and the induced metric.
Equivalently, compute in the projected primal quotient lattice. The normal
generators must not be substituted for tangent generators.
\end{definition}

For actual integral faces the local Euler--Maclaurin formula expresses
\(c_k\) as the sum of their normalized volumes times these local values.
The precise analytic inputs are \cite[Propositions 12--13, Definition 22,
Corollary 23, Theorem 26]{bv}: quotient and ambient/subspace compatibility,
dual solid subdivision additivity, and the local coefficient formula.
Section~\ref{sec:balance} gives the complete argument needed to use
simplicial constants without assuming the original polytope is simple or
full-dimensional.

\section{The complete refined normal cycle}\label{sec:balance}
The following construction works in \(L=\Z^m\) with a fixed rational
positive-definite metric. Let \(P\) be a nonempty bounded rational polytope
given by finitely many nonzero integral inward normals from a fixed list
\(N\). For coefficient statements assume that its Ehrhart counting function
is a polynomial, as it is for an integral polytope or an original hive.
The geometric refinement and balance lemmas themselves need no polynomiality
assumption. Fix \(1\leq k<m\), and put \(q=m-k\).

\begin{lemma}[Compatible refinement using original rows]\label{lem:fan}
The full ambient normal fan of \(P\) has a pointed simplicial refinement
\(\Gamma\) whose rays come from the original normal directions, including
when \(P\) has hidden affine equalities.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Replace each inequality \(b_r+\langle n_r,x\rangle\geq0\) by
\(b_r+\langle n_r,x\rangle+\delta a_r\geq0\), with all \(a_r>0\)
generic and \(\delta>0\) sufficiently small. The perturbed polytope is
full-dimensional, because any point of \(P\) now satisfies every row
strictly. It has the same zero recession cone and is bounded. Genericity
avoids the finite affine circuit degeneracies, so it is simple.

Every perturbed vertex is given by an independent \(m\)-row basis of
original inequalities. There are only finitely many such bases. For every
basis feasible for arbitrarily small positive \(\delta\), its vertex
has a finite limit in \(P\), with the same basis tight. That limit is
an actual vertex, and its normal cone contains the perturbed vertex cone.
A common sufficiently small \(\delta\) handles every feasible basis.
The perturbed normal fan therefore refines the full ambient fan of \(P\).
Parallel physical rows retain their separate offsets; genericity selects
at most one over a fixed oriented normal on a simple face.
No lattice count of the perturbed polytope is used.
\end{proof}

For \(\sigma\in\Gamma\) of dimension \(q\), assign weight
\(w_\sigma=\vol(F)\) when \(\relint\sigma\) lies in the normal cone
\(C_F\) of an actual \(k\)-face \(F\), with \(\dim C_F=q\), and
assign weight zero otherwise. Each top-dimensional subdivision cell of
\(C_F\) receives the same full actual face volume.

For a \((q-1)\)-cone \(\tau\), let \(u_{\sigma/\tau}\) denote the
primitive generator of the image of \(\sigma\) in
\(L^*/(L^*\cap\operatorname{span}\tau)\).
\begin{lemma}[Complete primitive balance]\label{lem:balance}
For every \((q-1)\)-cone \(\tau\) of \(\Gamma\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:balance}
 \sum_{\substack{\sigma\supset\tau\\\dim\sigma=q}}
 w_\sigma u_{\sigma/\tau}=0.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If \(\relint\tau\) lies in a coarse cone of dimension \(q-1\), that
cone corresponds to an actual \((k+1)\)-face \(G\). Each adjacent
nonzero-weight cell corresponds to one facet \(F\) of \(G\) near
\(\relint\tau\). Put \(M_G=L\cap\operatorname{lin}(G-G)\).
Restriction \(L^*\to M_G^*\) is surjective because \(M_G\) is saturated;
its kernel is \(L^*\cap\operatorname{span}\tau\). For the primitive
facet conormal \(u\),
\(\operatorname{covol}(M_F)=\operatorname{covol}(M_G)\|u\|\).
Indeed, extend a basis of its kernel by a lattice vector with \(u(v)=1\);
the extra perpendicular height is \(1/\|u\|\). The Euclidean facet
balance formula, divided by the common covolume, is exactly
\eqref{eq:balance}. An overall reversal from outward to inward normals
does not change the zero sum.

If \(\relint\tau\) lies inside a coarse \(q\)-cone, exactly two adjacent
\(q\)-cells in its span contribute. Their weights agree and their primitive
quotient directions are opposite. If it lies inside a coarse cone of larger
dimension, all adjacent \(q\)-cell weights are zero. These cases exhaust
the refinement, including coarse cones with lineality.
\end{proof}

For every independent \((q-1)\)-support \(J\), choose a rational linear
functional \(h_J\) on its quotient lattice, allowing the zero functional.
For \(I=J\cup\{i\}\), choose a saturated integer kernel basis \(M_J\)
of the original normal matrix. In its coordinates,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:primitive}
 u_{I/J}=\frac{M_J^T n_i}{\gcd\text{ of all coordinates of }M_J^T n_i}.
\end{equation}
Independence makes the denominator positive. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:beta}
 \beta_I=\alpha_I+\sum_{J\text{ a facet support of }I}h_J(u_{I/J}).
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Correction identity]\label{prop:identity}
For every such rational field \(h\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:identity}
 c_k(P)=\sum_{\substack{\sigma\in\Gamma\\\dim\sigma=q}}w_\sigma\alpha_\sigma
       =\sum_{\substack{\sigma\in\Gamma\\\dim\sigma=q}}w_\sigma\beta_\sigma.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
First dilate to make \(P\) integral. On each actual normal cone, BV dual
solid valuation adds the local constants of all top-dimensional simplicial
cells; lower-dimensional intersections contribute zero in that fixed span.
For a nonpointed coarse cone, ambient/subspace compatibility identifies the
value with the transverse value in the actual affine hull. The local
Euler--Maclaurin formula gives the first equality. Insert
\eqref{eq:beta} and interchange the finite sums. The coefficient of every
functional \(h_J\) is the complete sum \eqref{eq:balance}, so all
corrections cancel. Transfer back using \eqref{eq:scaling}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:all-support}
If \(\beta_I\geq\eps>0\) on every independent \(q\)-support, then
\(c_k(P)\geq\eps\sum_{\dim F=k}\vol(F)\).
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
All weights are nonnegative. Every actual \(k\)-face has at least one
top-dimensional normal subdivision cell. Sum \eqref{eq:identity}; use
\eqref{eq:scaling} for rational \(P\).
\end{proof}
This is a sufficient field criterion. Failure to find such a field does
not exhibit a polytope, much less a whole LR polynomial, with a negative
coefficient. Abstract nonnegative balanced weights need not be realizable
face-volume weights.

\section{Realizability pruning}\label{sec:pruning}
Consider a homogeneous polyhedral family
\(P_b=\{x:\rho_r(b,x)\geq0\}\), where each \(\rho_r\) is linear in
the combined boundary and interior coordinates. Let \(C\) be the cone
of all \((b,x)\) satisfying the physical inequalities; a larger cone
containing every permitted family member is also safe. For a physical row
set \(S\), set
\[
 C_S=C\cap\{\rho_r=0:r\in S\},\qquad
 \cl(S)=\{r:\rho_r\text{ vanishes identically on }C_S\}.
\]
Given independent normal directions \(I\), a \emph{physical branch}
chooses one original row above each direction. Retain \(I\) if at least
one such branch \(S\) has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:retained}
 \rank\{n_r:r\in\cl(S)\}=|I|.
\end{equation}
For a specified boundary this is a necessary support condition. The converse
below is existential: every retained physical branch occurs at some legal
integral boundary in some compatible refinement. It does not specify the
same boundary or refinement for different branches.

\begin{lemma}[Support of positive weights]\label{lem:support}
Every \(q\)-cell of the refinement in Lemma~\ref{lem:fan} with positive
weight has a retained physical branch.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(\sigma\) contribute the volume of an actual \(k\)-face \(F\).
Choose its \(q\) defining physical rows in the relaxed polytope, and an
incident relaxed vertex whose independent \(m\)-row basis contains them.
Its limit is an actual vertex \(v\) of \(P_b\), with these rows tight.
For \(\xi\in\relint\sigma\subset\relint C_F\), minimization at \(v\)
shows \(v\in F\). Each selected normal belongs to \(C_F\), so its row
is constant on \(F\) and, being zero at \(v\), is tight on all of \(F\).

Let \(T(F)\) consist of all original rows tight on \(F\), including
every hidden affine equality. Then
\(S\subset T(F)\) and \(\rank_x T(F)=m-\dim F=q\).
A relative-interior point \((b,x)\) of \(F\) lies in \(C_S\).
Every row in \(\cl(S)\) vanishes there. Its restriction to \(F\) is a
nonnegative affine function, so vanishing at a relative-interior point
forces it to vanish identically. Therefore
\[
 S\subset\cl(S)\subset T(F),\qquad
 q\leq\rank_x\cl(S)\leq q.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:retained} for the actual branch. Taking an existential
choice over all branches cannot discard it.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Family-dependent sign certificate]\label{thm:pruned}
If the field in \eqref{eq:beta} has \(\beta_I\geq\eps>0\) for every
retained independent \(q\)-support, then every nonempty period-one member
of the family satisfies
\[
 c_k(P_b)\geq\eps\sum_{\dim F=k}\vol(F).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:identity} holds for every field, regardless of sign.
Lemma~\ref{lem:support} puts every positive-weight cell in the retained
set. Excluded cells have weight zero and impose no sign condition.
Corollary~\ref{cor:all-support}'s final summation and rational scaling now
apply without altering any incidence or balance identity.
\end{proof}

For rank six, the universal cone uses 28 heights and all 45 original rhombi.
Its affine lineality has dimension three. Set
\(h(0,0)=h(1,0)=h(0,1)=0\); subtracting an affine height function gives
this gauge and leaves every rhombus slack unchanged. The resulting cone is
pointed and full-dimensional in 25 coordinates. Its 166 primitive extreme
rays provide an exact closure test: intersect the ray sets on which every
selected physical row is tight, and find every row vanishing on all the
surviving rays. If there is no surviving ray, the face is the origin and
every row belongs to its closure. Cone completeness must be verified; an
incomplete ray list could make this exclusion unsound.
Section~\ref{sec:closure-calculus} gives an alternative complete closure
certificate from feasible vectors and positive identities. Their 166 tight
masks agree exactly with the original classifier, and all six physical-row
symmetries preserve this set of masks. Either complete route certifies the
closure predicate used below.

For nine independent normals, compute an exact nonzero kernel vector
\(w\in\Q^{10}\). Condition~\eqref{eq:retained} is equivalent to every
closure row being orthogonal to \(w\). All branches over the three repeated
directions are checked. The six barycentric coordinate permutations preserve
physical rows, the cone, the original lattice and its metric. Thus the
predicate is invariant under that six-element group.

\section{Exact scalar computation and its acceleration}\label{sec:scalar}
Let \(N\) be any independent \(q\)-row normal matrix. In normal-image
coordinates use
\[
 \Lambda=N\Z^{10},\quad I=[\Z^q:\Lambda],\quad
 G=NN^T,\quad Q=G^{-1}.
\]
Let \(d_i\) be the least positive integer with \(d_i e_i\in\Lambda\),
and \(\mathcal F=\Lambda\cap\prod_i[0,d_i)\). Unique reduction modulo
the primitive axes gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:series}
 S(c)=\frac{\sum_{p\in\mathcal F}e^{\langle c,p\rangle}}
 {\prod_i(1-e^{d_i c_i})},\qquad
 |\mathcal F|=\frac{\prod_i d_i}{I}.
\end{equation}
Every fundamental numerator point is needed. Equal Gram matrices and equal
indices alone need not identify the full image lattice. Type reuse must
verify its original columns and exact row/ambient transport.

Let \(R_Q\) be the degree-preserving holomorphic projection for metric
\(Q\). The local constant is
\(\alpha_N=R_Q(S^{[0]})\), where \([0]\) means total homogeneous degree
zero \cite{bvasym,gpz}. This projection precedes any one-variable
specialization. A Laurent constant on a generic ray is a different operation.
With \(B_a(x)\) the Bernoulli polynomial in
\(ze^{xz}/(e^z-1)=\sum_{a\geq0}B_a(x)z^a/a!\), put
\[
 W(a)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal F}\prod_i
 \frac{d_i^{a_i}B_{a_i}(p_i/d_i)}{a_i!}.
\]
Then the following finite formula specifies every scalar without a cache:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:direct}
 \alpha_N=\frac{(-1)^q}{\prod_i d_i}
 \sum_{\substack{a\in\Z_{\geq0}^q\\|a|=q}}
 W(a)\,T\bigl(\{i:a_i=0\},(\max(a_i-1,0))_i\bigr).
\end{equation}
Here \(T(\varnothing,0)=1\). If \(j\notin U\), \(e_j>0\) and
\(|e|=|U|\), set \(b=Q_{UU}^{-1}Q_{Uj}\) and use
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projection}
 T(U,e)=\sum_{i\in U}b_iT(U\setminus\{i\},e-\mathbf e_j).
\end{equation}
To prove it, split \(c_j\) into its projection on the denominator span
and its orthogonal remainder. In every remainder term, expand the other
factors into the same two spaces. The denominator-space factor has negative
homogeneous degree, whose holomorphic projection is zero. Orthogonal
multiplicativity kills that term. The projected part cancels one denominator
and yields \eqref{eq:projection}. Induction finishes the proof.

An independent implementation uses the full Euler--Maclaurin recurrence.
For \(T\) a row subset, write \(I_T=[\Z^T:N_T\Z^{10}]\) and
\(G_T=N_TN_T^T\), with \(I_\varnothing=\alpha_\varnothing=1\).
Choose a common generic vector \(w\), and set
\(c_T=G_T^{-1}w_T\). With \(\mu_\varnothing=1\), the recurrence is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:forward}
 \mu_T(s)=S_T(sc_T)
 -\sum_{\varnothing\ne U\subseteq T}
 \frac{(-1)^{|U|}I_{T\setminus U}}
 {I_Ts^{|U|}\prod_{i\in U}c_{T,i}}\,
 \mu_{T\setminus U}(s).
\end{equation}
The quotient covectors are compatible restrictions of the same specialization;
all negative Laurent coefficients cancel. Multiplying by \(s^{|T|}\)
reduces scalar extraction to finite polynomial arithmetic. Higher Taylor
coefficients needed by larger supports are retained to the required degree.

\subsection{Odd dimension and exceptional lattice cosets}
For odd \(q\), proper-subset constants can replace a full degree-\(q\)
jet calculation. Put \(\ell(c)=\sum_i c_i\), and for \(F\subseteq T\)
define
\[
 K(\varnothing)=1,\quad
 a(F)=Q_{FF}^{-1}(Q\mathbf1)_F,\quad
 K(F)=\sum_{i\in F}a_i(F)K(F\setminus\{i\}).
\]
Nested orthogonal projection proves the last recurrence by cancelling one
numerator factor. If \(\mathbf1\in\Lambda\), closed/open reciprocity
gives \(S(-c)=-e^{\ell(c)}S(c)\). Apply \(R_Q\), insert the complete
local recurrence, and compare degree zero. A proper germ of positive
degree is orthogonal to the denominator variables and has zero contribution;
the surviving expression is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd}
 A_T=-\frac12\sum_{J\subsetneq T}
 (-1)^{|T\setminus J|}\frac{I_J}{I_T}\,
 \alpha_J\frac{K(T\setminus J)}{|T\setminus J|!},
 \qquad \alpha_T=A_T.
\end{equation}
Thus at \(q=9\) there are 511 proper terms, and only lower constants are
required. Original lattice membership is essential.

For general \(\Lambda\), let \(S_a\) be the closed orthant sum on
\(a+\Lambda\), and \(D=S_0-S_{-\mathbf1}\). The two coset reciprocity
identities give \(D(-c)=e^{\ell(c)}D(c)\), so instead
\begin{equation}\label{eq:defect}
 \alpha_T=A_T+\tfrac12R_Q(D^{[0]}).
\end{equation}
Let \(\widehat H\) be the character group of \(H=\Z^q/\Lambda\).
For \(z_i=\chi(e_i)\), character orthogonality makes the defect explicit:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:characters}
 D(c)=\frac1I\sum_{\chi\in\widehat H}
 \left(1-\prod_i z_i\right)\prod_i(1-z_i e^{c_i})^{-1}.
\end{equation}
All characters, their actual axis phases and the factor \(1/I\) are
retained. Terms with \(\prod_i z_i=1\) vanish. The other terms are
evaluated exactly in a cyclotomic field, or by complete rational Galois
traces followed by \eqref{eq:projection}.

For independent primitive rows with entries in \(\{0,1,-1\}\), a
nontrivial character cannot have only one nontrivial coordinate. If it
has exactly two, column annihilation forces identical row supports and
phases equal or opposite. Independence rules out a single relative sign;
both signs force order two, with both phases \(-1\). Their product is
one, so the defect vanishes. Every surviving character therefore has at
least three nontrivial phases. Its coordinate poles number at most \(q-3\),
which is six at \(q=9\). This is the scalar acceleration used in the
large certificate; it does not assert positivity of the raw scalars.

\section{Finite certificates and assembly}\label{sec:finite}
All finite tests use exact integers and rationals. Floating optimization
proposes a field only; it contributes no sign decision to the proof.
The six coordinate isometries act on complete original supports. Averaging
a field seeded at support-orbit representatives includes every stabilizer:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:field}
 \beta_I=\alpha_I+\frac{B_I n}{6D}.
\end{equation}
Formula~\eqref{eq:field} is the orbit-averaged convention for the
\(c_1,\ldots,c_5\) fields. The \(c_6\) certificate instead lists its
381 literal support functionals directly, with denominator \(10^{12}\),
and uses \eqref{eq:beta} without an additional averaging factor.
At each support, the kernel matrix \(M\) has an integer left inverse
\(A\) satisfying \(NM=0\) and \(AM=I\), and the expected rational
rank, or equivalently verifies full rank and gcd one of its maximal minors.
This proves that its column lattice is the full saturated kernel.
Every restricted conormal is divided by the gcd of all its coordinates.
The complete original-normal action is checked against any stored sparse
matrix action; agreement of only floating residuals is insufficient.

\section{Verification contract and reproducibility}\label{sec:verification}
A certificate is the rational field together with the exact finite domain
and the mathematical interpretation above. A file hash establishes identity;
it does not prove a scalar value or a domain complete. The distribution must
therefore distinguish the following operations.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Reconstruct the original rhombi, lattice and symmetry; verify the
universal cone and all physical-branch closure tests.
\item Regenerate all required normal supports, saturated kernels, image
lattices, type transports and primitive incidences. Reject omissions,
duplicates, foreign rows, and incomplete tails.
\item Reconstruct every local scalar from the stated analytic recurrence,
including its complete numerator and exceptional lattice cosets. A
proper-subset cache must itself have a complete analytic verification.
\item Evaluate every corrected value exactly and compare it with its
declared bound. Reconstruct the original action independently of any
stored sparse matrix. Record exact minima and complete domain counts.
\item Verify corrupt-data and omission controls, process exit, and complete
output before accepting a pass. Reproduce from the distributed source/data
copy, not from paths in the private research workspace.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Formal verification}
No Lean, Coq or HOL verification of the whole theorem is claimed. A useful
formalization must connect the concrete LR coefficient to the original hive
count, prove the polynomial/coefficient interpretation, formalize the
saturated-lattice normal-cycle and pruning arguments, and certify the exact
finite computation. Proving only that a nonnegative weighted sum is
nonnegative while assuming this entire connection would leave the central
obligation untouched. The accompanying formalization assessment separates
these obligations and identifies the exact certificate predicates suitable
for a small kernel-checked arithmetic layer.
The new closure certificate offers a concrete first target: Lean's standard
library has a sound LRAT checker to which the RUP additions can be exported
with ordered unit-propagation hints. That route has been investigated but
not compiled or kernel-checked here. A successful finite CNF check would
still require the formula interpretation, closure sandwich and remaining
LR/BV bridge to be formalized.

\section{Mathematical and computational implications}
Realizability pruning is available for any homogeneous fixed-row polyhedral
family with the local coefficient formula. Its utility in another family
depends on proving the original support implication, computing a complete
closure description, and finding an exact retained field. The rank-six
success does not establish that these tasks remain tractable in higher rank.

The odd scalar formula and finite-character defect are also reusable beyond
this instance, subject to their precise lattice and metric hypotheses.
The primitive \(0,\pm1\) row condition explains the six-pole defect bound;
it cannot be transferred to arbitrary integer matrices without checking
the corresponding character supports.

The proof does not establish a positive tableau formula, monotonicity,
concavity, superadditivity, or a simple local rule for paying each negative
edge. Such statements are different from coefficient positivity, and some
proposed monotonicity-related shortcuts were independently refuted during
the investigation. A smaller conceptual field or a more local correction
would improve explanation and verification cost. Those are substantive
further questions, rather than missing premises of the stated certificate
once its complete checks pass.
